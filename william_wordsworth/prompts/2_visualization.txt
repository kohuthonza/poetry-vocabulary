Propose vocabulary whose meaning can be illustrated concretely and without substantial ambiguity. The purpose is to give me a useful mental image of things with which I may have no direct experience, especially unfamiliar animals and plants.

Selection preferences:
- Prioritize animals and plants, and unfamiliar specific buildings or places that can be represented clearly.
- Exclude feelings, emotions, abstract concepts, figurative ideas, and terms requiring speculative or symbolic imagery.
- Exclude landscape terms.

For a word with several senses, identify the concrete sense being illustrated, using its explanation and available corpus context. Give a compact proposal table containing the row number, exact English Word, intended visual subject/sense, and whether an existing resolved set can be reused or a new set is needed. 3. NAMING NEW SETS

For an approved term without a resolved set, derive the lexical part of the basename from the complete English Word value in column 1. Never derive it from a substring, translation, alternate modern spelling, or scientific label selected during research.

Lowercase the lexical spelling; turn spaces and hyphen separators into underscores; remove filename punctuation; collapse repeated underscores; remove leading/trailing underscores. Preserve spelling and inflection, including historical forms. Then prepend the literal prefix ww-.

Examples:
- grunsel -> ww-grunsel
- peat-fire -> ww-peat_fire
- a multiword term -> ww-a_multiword_term

The CSV stores only the basename, such as ww-peat_fire: no path, number, extension, HTML, or filename list.

4. FIND APPROPRIATE EXISTING IMAGES

Use publicly accessible real photographs or suitable existing illustrations. Do not generate substitute imagery. Prefer relevant botanical, ornithological, museum, scientific, and other reliable sources, including well-documented photographic collections.

For animals and plants, prefer photographs from the relevant place when known. For a Cumbrian passage, prefer suitable Cumbrian examples, then British examples, while prioritizing correct identification and a clearly visible subject. For passages set elsewhere, use that locality where appropriate. Do not substitute a local species for a clearly different named species. Record significant identification choices and any use of representative specimens from another region. These illustrate vocabulary; they are not proof of the exact historical specimen intended by the poet.

Choose useful subject detail and reasonable variety, such as flowers/leaves/whole plant or different animal poses. Avoid distant or obscured subjects, wrong species, unreadable pictures, and duplicate copies or crops of the same photograph. Tether images must visibly show the restraint, not merely an animal. Peat-fire means domestic peat fuel, not a peatland wildfire. Stop selecting once five suitable, distinct images pass the checks; do not keep replacing satisfactory images for marginal aesthetic gains.

Prefer adequately sized source renditions over tiny thumbnails. Record the source-page URL, direct downloaded-image URL, retrieval date, and available authorship/licence information in the compact index format defined below. Do not write a separate report for each image. Preserve existing credits and watermarks. Public access alone is not a reuse licence. Do not invent missing attribution or licensing information.

If an image is too small or unclear, obtain a better source instead of enlarging it. Record the pixel dimensions once in the source table; they describe the downloaded website rendition, not a camera/master original unless that is established. Note any orientation-related dimension swap briefly as an exception. Existing SVG illustrations may be obtained as suitable website raster renditions and converted, with the downloaded format recorded accurately.

Decode each completed JPEG to verify its format and dimensions. Check distinctness and inspect all NEW selected images visually as described below. Trust previously resolved sets unless there is concrete contrary evidence.
