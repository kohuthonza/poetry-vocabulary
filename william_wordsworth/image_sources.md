# William Wordsworth visual vocabulary

190 JPEG images for 38 resolved vocabulary entries across Prelude Books 1–13. The headerless CSVs use Image basename in column 10; a blank field means no image. Original vocabulary fields are preserved.

## Files and future additions

All sets below are resolved, with files numbered 0–4. Keep the `ww-` prefix and existing basenames stable. Reuse a set for the same whole term and meaning, including the listed aliases. Later images use the next unused number, such as `ww-alder-5.jpg`; the CSV basename stays unchanged and the Anki renderer must support the available count.

All files are actual JPEGs, converted at quality 95 without cropping, resizing or upscaling. Dimensions describe the downloaded website rendition, not an unavailable master original. Transparency was composited onto white where needed. Images were decoded and visually reviewed; credits and watermarks were retained. There were no width/height swaps.

Book 2: steed illustrates a riding horse with British riding scenes, representative New Forest ponies, and a Thoroughbred at the Maryland State Fair (US); chamois illustrates Rupicapra rupicapra in the Alps, not leather or cycling padding. Downloaded and saved dimensions are identical; no orientation changes.

Oat details and some woodcock photographs come from elsewhere in Europe. Grunsel images 2 and 3 show yellow flowerheads. Peat-fire shows domestic peat fuel; knave shows playing cards.

Book 3: hawthorn uses British and Northern Irish photographs; popinjays uses representative parrots, including a captive African grey in England, scarlet macaws in Ecuador and Honduras, and a captive white-fronted amazon. These do not identify a particular species intended by Wordsworth. The Commons source for popinjays-1 describes a parrot without specifying its species. Retrieved 11 September 2026; all ten downloaded and saved dimensions are identical, with no orientation changes. Each JPEG was re-encoded at quality 95; no crop or resize was applied.

Book 4: ash includes European tree, foliage/fruit and bud photographs plus a historical botanical plate; hazel includes a British nursery specimen, a historical botanical illustration, British catkins, a leaf/nut specimen and Austrian catkins; badger includes British wildlife photography and representative European specimens, including a controlled setting. These illustrate vocabulary, not exact historical specimens. Retrieved 12 September 2026. All 15 files are JPEGs converted at quality 95, with downloaded dimensions preserved and no orientation changes. All three sets are resolved; available files are 0–4 for each basename.

Book 5: heifer uses source-identified British heifers. Brood shows hens and ducks with their young, including British/Northern Irish examples and representative domestic chickens from elsewhere (including Hebron). Gossamer shows British spider silk on grass, vegetation and ironwork. Pinfold includes the Cumbrian enclosures at Hartley and Bolton and examples at Barlow, Heckington and Laxton. Retrieved 12 September 2026; downloaded dimensions preserved; no orientation swaps.

Book 6: cloister shows Gloucester, Durham, Lacock and Westminster, including a historical Durham photograph. Elm illustrates wych elm (Ulmus glabra), with British tree, foliage and bud photographs and an Estonian flower detail from Keila. Eglantine illustrates sweetbrier (Rosa rubiginosa), with flowers, foliage and hips from the Netherlands, Germany and Austria. These representative specimens do not identify the exact trees or roses intended by Wordsworth. Retrieved 13 September 2026; downloaded dimensions preserved; no orientation swaps.

Book 7: redbreast uses British European robins, including Rydal, Cumbria. Ermine shows winter-coated stoats and ceremonial fur: a Serbian royal garment detail, a historical fashion engraving, and a portrait of a British nobleman in coronation robes. These are representative vocabulary illustrations. Retrieved 13 September 2026. Downloaded dimensions preserved.

Book 9: barn swallow and migratory locust are representative species, not identifications of exact historical specimens. Locust sources include captive specimens in Germany and wild specimens in Italy, Greece and Japan; individual localities are documented on source pages.

Book 10: Scots pine represents the tree meaning included in the CSV, not the passage’s verb meaning (to long sorrowfully). Scottish specimens are preferred; the mature-cone and needle close-up is from Vinnytsia district, Ukraine. Retrieved 18 September 2026. Downloaded dimensions preserved; no crops or resizing.
